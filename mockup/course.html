The AI toolbox and playground

Who is this course for?

This is a set of learning resources for teachers of second, foreign and additional languages, including teachers involved in heritage language education, who want to develop their skills in using artificial intelligence in their classes.

How is the course organised?

The course consists of three parts.

  • Part 1 provides teachers with some background theory about artificial intelligence for language education. This may be especially useful for teachers who are interested in deepening their understanding of how AI is reshaping language education.
  • Part 2 offers concrete and practical guidance for using artificial intelligence to support learning activities that focus on different aspects of language lessons. Here, teachers can find practical videos as well as instructions for using various AI tools. This section will be most relevant to teachers who are looking for ideas on how to use specific tools.
  • Part 3 is a collection of examples of language activities and lessons, organised by levels of linguistic proficiency (with a special section devoted to Content and Language Integrated Lessons). This can be used both as a source of inspiration and for reflection on how the AI Lang guidelines might be applied.
How can you use the course?

This course has been designed with flexible use in mind. Its modular structure is intended to help teachers quickly locate the resources that are most relevant to their needs and interests. This means that teachers can engage with any module in the course in any order they wish.

For teachers who prefer a more systematic approach, the modules have been sequenced to build upon each other.

The tools and services that are presented in the course are intended as illustrative examples of how AI can support planning, materials design, teaching, and feedback. They were selected because, at the time of writing, they were widely accessible and free of charge (at least for restricted use or trial versions), making them suitable for teachers who want to experiment. Users should be aware that features, pricing and user interfaces may change over time. Teachers should check each service’s terms and data protection policies before using it with learners. The ECML does not endorse the use of any specific service.
