The AI Lang Guidelines

Who are the AI Lang guidelines for?

This guidance is most directly addressed to teachers of second and foreign languages, as well as teachers involved in heritage language education, who can use the framework to problematise their practice and focus their professional development.

Some other audiences include:

(a) teacher educators, who can use the framework to extend and enrich language teacher professional development, and to inform assessment in language teacher education

(b) policy planners and administrators, who may find the framework useful in developing accountability structures for the use of AI-assisted language education.

The ultimate beneficiaries are language learners, including especially learners who are vulnerable, or may be rendered vulnerable through the uncritical adoption of AI-assisted practices.

How is the framework organised?

The framework is hierarchically organised. At its core lie four principles (e.g. “The use of AI in language education should be purposeful”), which situate the framework within a broader commitment to humanistic and critically informed language education, and anchor the guidance to the core values of democracy, human rights, and multilingualism. The principles are the highest level of abstraction in the framework, and are expected to remain relevant even in the face of technological change.

Each principle is operationalised into two guidelines (e.g., “Safeguard the data security and digital safety of all participants”), which give the principles practical shape. Like the principles, the guidelines are intended to be broadly applicable across different educational contexts and levels, and to retain their relevance as the technological landscape continues to evolve.

From each guideline, we derive a set of several competence descriptors. These descriptors define what ethical AI use looks like in observable, practitioner-level terms. Each descriptor can be formulated in question format (e.g., “To what extent do the AI-assisted tasks I design increase learner agency?”), to avoid the binary framing implicit in ‘I can…’ statements. Unlike the principles and guidelines, the competence descriptors are context-sensitive, and teachers are encouraged to prioritise those that are most directly relevant to their role, their learners, and their institutional setting.

Each descriptor is further illustrated by reflection questions, which invite teachers to connect the framework to the particularities of their own practice (e.g., “Does the AI tool you use require learners to sign in? If so, where can you find out what data it collects?”). The function of the reflection questions is not to elicit predetermined correct answers, but to scaffold the kind of professional judgment, critical awareness and contextual sensitivity that responsible AI use requires. They can be used for individual reflection, for structured professional dialogue with colleagues, or as a basis for participatory discussion with learners and other stakeholders.

The use of AI, as described in the framework, should be understood as a holistic ethical practice. The principles and guidelines that make up the framework are presented separately for reasons of analytical convenience, but they overlap conceptually (e.g., considerations of social justice cannot be disentangled from guidance on safety). The guidelines therefore should be read as showing differences in nuance and emphasis rather than categorical distinctions.

How can you use the framework?

The guidance in this framework is intended to provide language teachers, teacher educators and administrators with a set of reflective tools, which they can use to develop contextually appropriate practice. It does not constitute a compliance checklist or an inventory of conditions that must be fulfilled in order to integrate artificial intelligence into language education.

The reflection questions that are included under each guideline are central to this perspective. Teachers are encouraged to use and adapt these as necessary to help scaffold structured individual reflection, collaborative professional dialogue, and, where appropriate, participatory discussions with learners, school leaders, data protection officers, IT personnel and other stakeholders.

These questions are not meant to elicit predetermined (“correct”) answers. Rather, they serve to support informed professional judgment by surfacing assumptions, clarifying values, and making explicit the pedagogical, ethical, and institutional considerations that shape decisions about AI use.

The four principles of the framework

AI-assisted language education should be safe.

All participants in education (learners, teachers and institutions) must be protected from actual or potential harm, both immediate and long-term, when engaging in AI-supported language learning.

Are our uses of artificial intelligence in language education legally compliant, transparent and low-risk?

Guideline 1Comply with existing legal requirements and institutional standards.Guideline 2Safeguard the data security and digital safety of all participants.

AI-assisted language education should be socially and environmentally responsible.

This principle positions artificial intelligence in language education as a force for social good, by fostering linguistic and cultural diversity, and by minimising environmental harm and social injustice.

Does our use of AI position our teaching as a force for positive change?

Guideline 3Promote social justice, linguistic and cultural diversity.Guideline 4Foster environmental sustainability.

AI-assisted language education should be pedagogically purposeful.

Focusing on the pedagogical integration of artificial intelligence, this principle entails that AI should create affordances for teaching and learning that would not be possible, or would be prohibitively demanding in resources, without the use of such technology.

How does AI add genuine pedagogical value and improve the quality of teaching and learning?

Guideline 5Enhance theory- and policy-informed language pedagogy.Guideline 6Create expanded language learning opportunities.

AI-assisted language education should be human-centred.

What this principle indexes is that both language learners and language teachers can draw on existing humanistic traditions in language education, together with the affordances of artificial intelligence, in order to become more empowered.

How can teachers and learners use AI reflectively and maintain professional agency?

Guideline 7Prepare learners for critical, healthy engagement with AI.Guideline 8Empower language teachers as reflective professionals.
